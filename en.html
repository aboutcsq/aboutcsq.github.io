Local Government Finance2026-2027

Offshore Bonds Issued by Chinese LGFVs: Motives and Risks

This project examines why Chinese local government financing vehicles (LGFVs) borrow offshore and how debt risks arise. Its literature review covers local government finance and debt defaults. The materials include bond offering documents from the Singapore Exchange and results of auctions for government cash deposits. The district- and county-level data cover 435 announcements.

Research Materials

Local Financing
Pressure to roll over bank loans pushed local governments toward nonbank financing. Rising local debt crowded out private firms’ investment, and some cities used LGFV land purchases to support the land market and public financing.
Guarantees and Defaults
Investment fell more sharply at financially constrained state-owned firms after implicit guarantees weakened. Selective defaults by local governments were also linked to creditor banks’ political influence.
Offshore Issuance
Interest rate differentials between domestic and overseas markets were associated with US dollar bond issuance by Chinese firms. Some firms in high-risk sectors used the proceeds for intercompany lending. Spreads on Chinese corporate dollar bonds narrowed after China issued US dollar sovereign bonds.
Issue Pricing
Underwriter rebates and purchases of bonds they underwrote can raise issue prices. Regulatory comment letters lowered issuance success rates. Investors with prior business ties bought a larger share, contributing to issuance overpricing.

2025 · Research Project

Organizational Ethical Climate, Organizational Identification, and Sophisticated Self-Interest

The study collected survey data and used SPSS and AMOS to examine how organizational ethical climate and organizational identification relate to sophisticated self-interest. It offered three recommendations, including raising awareness of organizational rules.
